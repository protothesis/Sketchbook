# Dusting, 2026-10-27

Designed in chat. First pass built 2026-10-27 (`app/walk/dust.js`;
PLAY.md has the numbers it shipped with): everything under "First pass"
below. Cobwebs and the clean-room bonus aren't built.

The problem: walk mode is almost entirely passive. You walk to the few
things that exist, rooms feel empty, and pickups are scarce. A short run of
Voidigo brought back what's missing: break the things that shimmer, and
little shards scatter out, react as you approach, and get swept up. Lots of
small, juicy feedback. The question was how to get that feeling without
breaking anything, and the answer is to make restoration just as juicy.

Dusting is restoration that reacts every time you swing. It also makes the
theme literal: entropy is disorder, and dust is what disorder looks like.
You sweep it up and it feeds the light.

## The loop

1. Rooms have dust on their surfaces.
2. Select the **feather duster** and swing it (F). Each swing kicks up a
   cloud and thins the dust it hits.
3. Sometimes **entropy chits** fly out. Walk through them to collect them;
   they drift toward you over a short distance.
4. Chits fill a **pre-pool**. When it's full, it pours into the lamp.

## Decided

**Dust**

- A thin plane a little above the surface it covers, light grey, with an
  ordered **pixel dither** pattern scaled up and drawn nearest-neighbour.
- Laid out on the 1x-block grid (four cells per dungeon tile), on any
  reachable surface: floors, and the tops of blocks and ledges. Placed as
  both small patches and larger connected areas.
- **Thickness comes from the dither.** Dust has one to three layers, and a
  layer is a dither threshold. Each swipe removes a layer, so the pattern
  visibly thins. A light film takes one swipe; thick dust in a dusty region
  takes two or three.
- **Cleaned dust stays gone.** The layout comes from the room's seed; the
  save only records which cells you've cleared. Nothing can be farmed, and
  a clean room counts as restored, like a block on its mark.

**The duster**

- An item you select and swing with F, like the jars. It's in your
  inventory from the start for now (see "Later" for introducing it).
- **One press is one swing.** No hold-to-repeat. If it feels too mashy,
  widen the swing arc or hit area first.
- A swing hits the cells in an arc in front of you. Jumping and swinging
  reaches higher.

**Clouds**

- Chunky dust clouds that take a while to clear, then break down into
  finer motes that hang around and catch the light: they glow near your
  lamp and sconces and fade in the dark.
- Atmosphere only. They don't block your view or do anything else.

**Chits**

- Small sprites or particles, not 3D objects like the shard pickup.
- A short magnet range, and a small reaction as you approach.
- **The yield is random, including zero.** A zero still gets the full
  cloud, so it never feels like a dud. A hidden pity counter stops more
  than about three zeros in a row.

**Pre-pool**

- Chits fill a small meter. A full meter pours **one shard (⅓ charge)**
  into the lamp. That's the gain that starts the lamp's grace, so steady
  dusting can't hold the drain off forever, which it would if every chit
  counted as a gain.
- Pouring into the lamp should be a moment of its own, for example the
  meter clicking over into the lamp.
- **When you faint, the pre-pool drops on the lost path** as chits you
  pick up again when you retrace.

**Mood sets how much dust**

| Dust | Moods |
|---|---|
| Heavy: most surfaces, thick | dusty |
| Medium | still, hollow, warm |
| Light: patches | echoing, tilted, humming |
| None | damp, cold, breathing |

This is a starting table to tune. There's no "frozen" mood; `cold` stands
in for it.

**Shards stay, as finds**

The 3D shard stays as the bigger find, and chits are the steady trickle.
**Shards are never under objects, only under dust.** Lifting a block or
pot never pays anything, so there's no reason to pick up everything in a
room and put it back the way you would in Zelda. Pushing a block onto its
mark still pays, as it does today: that's moving an object to restore it.

## First pass

- Dust (dither plane, thickness, mood table, clearing saved per cell).
- The feather duster, clouds and motes.
- Chits with magnet range, the random yield, the pre-pool, and pre-pool
  chits dropping on the lost path.
- **Dormant crystals crusted in dust.** A crystal somewhere in a room,
  crusted over and dull, with a faint glint through the dither when your
  lamp light reaches it. This is Voidigo's "things that shimmer", so you
  go where the glint is rather than poking at everything. The crust is
  thicker than ordinary dust, so it takes a few swipes. Dusting it off
  wakes it with a pulse, and then it's collected as today's shard. Most
  of the floor shards placed today become crusted crystals.

## Potentials (after the first pass)

- **Cobwebs.** In corners and high on walls, out of reach of a swing from
  the floor. You climb onto a block, or jump and swing. They pay more
  chits than dust, in a bigger burst. A web could follow its own version
  of the mood table (echoing and hollow rooms suit webs even though their
  dust is lighter). A **crystal wrapped in a web** would be the jackpot
  version, and the best reason to climb.
- **A clean-room bonus.** Clearing a room's last dust forms a crystal
  where the last dust was. It's a completion moment, like the last block
  reaching its mark, and a reason to finish a room rather than skim its
  easy patches.

## Pacing to start from

- The lamp burns a shard every `RULES.lampSeconds` (18 s at the time of
  writing, so a charge every 54 s).
- Target: dusting a typical medium room takes about 30 s and gives back
  about 3 to 4 shards, against under 2 burned. That's clearly ahead, but
  not wildly. Dusty regions pay well.
- About 6 chits make a shard.
- Dusting within a lit sconce's shelter is pure gain, which makes a loop:
  light the sconce, then tidy the room.
- With this much more entropy coming in, sconces and waystones will
  probably feel cheap. Expect to raise their costs, or the drain,
  afterwards.

All of this goes in a tuning table (for example `DUST`) so the dev panel's
sliders pick it up.

## Later

- **Depth sets what you meet, not item unlocks.** Distance from the root
  sets what a room can contain, so the layout decides the order you learn
  things in: dust, crusted crystals and push blocks near the root; sconces
  mattering and the first cobwebs a few rooms in; wells, vessels, pixies
  and the hourglass deeper. You always have a way to get entropy. It needs
  its own design pass; until then everything is in your inventory from the
  start.
- **Audio.** There's none yet, and it's coming soon. Dusting needs a
  handful of sounds: swipe, puff, chit tick, the meter pouring into the
  lamp, a crystal waking.
- Dust that shows footprints, and dust that slowly comes back on routes
  you never walk (GAME-DESIGN §11, walked paths). It would only ever be a
  bonus, never a cost.
- Sweeping with a broom, as GAME-DESIGN §3 lists.
